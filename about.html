    Scientific Research

    My research connects galaxy-cluster physics with machine learning and statistical inference.  Since early work on dynamical mass estimation, I have studied how complex astronomical observations can inform masses, cosmology, and cluster structure—and how to examine what the algorithms learn along the way.

    With collaborators, I have used interpretability to uncover physical insight and evaluated the limits of transferring learned relationships between simulated environments.  My research program has received support from NASA's Astrophysics Theory Program and the NSF Astronomy and Astrophysics Research Grants program.

    Read the research →

    Institutional Leadership

    In STScI's Data Science Mission Office, I work across scientific research, data infrastructure, and strategy.  I have served twice as Acting Mission Head and have helped develop the Institute's approach to artificial intelligence.

    My contributions include drafting core responsible-AI guidelines with the AI Task Force, developing an AI strategy through conversations across the Institute, and establishing and running our AI Community of Practice.

    These roles require more than enthusiasm for a new technology.  They require identifying where it is useful, understanding its risks, and bringing people with different expertise into the decision.

    Teaching and Mentoring

    Before astronomy, I spent a decade teaching: middle-school Earth and space science, high-school physics, and university physics laboratories.  In Rwanda, I worked with high-school science teachers on engaging demonstrations using inexpensive, locally available materials.

    That background remains part of my scientific practice.  I initiated Hello Universe at MAST, developing its scope, vision, and prototype notebook; the MAST team developed and supports the published tutorials.  I also write about peer review, mentoring, and research-group AI policies.
